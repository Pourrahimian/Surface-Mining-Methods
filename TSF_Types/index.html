Condensed from the SME Tailings Management Handbook (2022) tables on containment, risks and water management shown in the lecture.

Every tonne of tailings leaves the mill with a lot of water. Dewatering at the plant (thickener, paste plant, filter) recovers some of it before deposition. Of what reaches the facility, part stays locked in the pores of the deposit and the rest is released to a pond or runoff. This explains where water management is concentrated in each facility type.

How it is calculated
  • Water per tonne of solids at a solids content Cw (% by weight) = (100 − Cw) ÷ Cw, taken as 1 t = 1 m³.
  • Recovered at the plant = water leaving the mill − water sent to the facility.
  • Saturated deposits (conventional, thickened, paste): pore water = 1/ρd − 1/Gs m³ per tonne, from the deposit's dry density and the solids specific gravity.
  • Filtered tailings are placed below saturation: pore water = placement moisture content (by dry mass).
  • Released at the facility = water sent − pore water. If that is negative, the deposit would not be saturated at that density (flagged).
  • Defaults are teaching values chosen inside the solids ranges from the lecture, not measured values. Evaporation, seepage and rainfall are left out.

In-pit TSFs are designed with wet or dry covers and can leave the waste fully or partially saturated. Reactive (sulphide-bearing) waste oxidizes when it has access to oxygen, which can produce acid rock drainage (ARD) and metal leaching. Below the water table, oxygen supply is very limited.
