Key ideas
  • The operating bench is extended toward the spoil by dumping key-cut waste against the highwall of the previous strip, the one the dragline sits on.
  • The key cut is made large enough to give enough competent material for the bench.
  • Dozers level the waste, so the dragline can stand closer to the spoil and cast further from the working bench.

Schematic, approximately to scale: 25 m overburden over a 4 m seam, 65° highwalls, 45° end faces, 37° spoil repose, 95 m boom at 32°. The key cut is enlarged (15 m floor, 70° inside face) to supply the bench, which extends about 18 m past the old highwall crest. In Section A–A, teal marks the bench material that the production cut digs again (rehandle) and slate marks the part that stays in place under the final-cut spoil. Section A–A looks north, so east is on the right, and it follows the dragline from stage to stage.
