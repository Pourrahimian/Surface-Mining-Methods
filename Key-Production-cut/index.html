Why a key cut?
  • Fewer excavation and highwall-stability problems.
  • A smaller average swing angle for the whole cut.
  • The production cut digs away from the new highwall, so the highwall is not damaged or undermined.

Schematic, approximately to scale: 25 m overburden over a 4 m seam, 65° highwalls, 45° end faces, 37° spoil repose, 95 m boom at 32°. The strip is about 64 m wide at the crest. The key cut has an 8 m floor and a 75° inside face, and takes about a third of the overburden. The previous strip’s coal has been removed, so its floor is open between the old highwall and the spoil from previous cuts. The Section A–A view looks north, so east is on the right, and it follows the dragline from stage to stage.
