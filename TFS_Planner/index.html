Assumptions and simplifications
  • Dry density already includes the pore space, so pore water is inside the tailings volume; only free water (pond + flood) is added on top.
  • Pond volume = daily slurry water (Tab 1) × days of storage. Flood volume = rainfall depth × footprint, with no run-on from outside the facility.
  • Vertical inner walls: real inner slopes and the tailings beach reduce capacity, so a real embankment would be higher.
  • When density varies, it rises linearly from the initial to the final value over the LOM and applies to the whole deposit. Final volume always uses the final density.
  • All default values are teaching values, not design values.

Assumptions and simplifications
  • Tailings volume and water storage come from Tabs 1–2. Water storage (pond + flood) and freeboard are carried from the first year.
  • Ring dike: square footprint from Tab 2 with vertical inner walls; fill volume = maximum section × crest perimeter (4 × side).
  • Cross-valley: V-shaped valley with constant side slope and floor gradient. Storage V = s·h³ ÷ (3g), so h = (3gV ÷ s)1/3. Fill volume integrates the section along the crest as the ground rises to the abutments.
  • Each stage is built at the start of its period, to the height needed at the end of the period plus freeboard. The first stage is the starter dyke.
  • Upstream raises keep the overall downstream slope and step upstream onto the beach; downstream raises extend the upstream face; centreline raises go straight up the starter crest; modified centreline shifts the crest upstream by the chosen H:V.
  • Raise-rate bands follow slide 28 and apply to upstream construction: < 4.5 m/yr most desirable, 4.5–9 m/yr upper end of desirable, > 15 m/yr can be hazardous.
  • Simplified geometry for teaching: no internal drains, filters, beach slope or foundation settlement.

Mount Tapian, Marinduque (slides 55–57): tailings were placed in a mined-out pit whose drainage tunnel had been sealed with a concrete plug. The plug sat under only ~30 m of rock but held back ~300 m of saturated tailings. On 24 March 1996 the rock around the plug broke.

Assumptions and simplifications
  • Fluid pressure on the plug P = ρgh, with h the head of saturated tailings/water above the tunnel. Slide 57 uses water (1000 kg/m³); raising the fluid density shows the effect of a heavier saturated slurry.
  • Rock confinement is the vertical overburden stress ρrghr. If the fluid pressure exceeds it, the rock can be jacked open (hydraulic fracturing) and the plug bypassed.
  • A real plug design also checks plug shear and bearing, grouting, seepage, and a factor of safety. This tab reproduces only the slide 57 comparison.
